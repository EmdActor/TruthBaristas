Blog 01

Truth vs. Opinion

This blog explores the difference between truth and opinion. What actually counts as truth, and why is it so different from an opinion? Is truth always more important, or can opinion carry its own kind of weight?

I sit by the window with a coffee in hand, watching the world outside my home bustle with wildlife, from the branches down to the ground. Then I wonder, if sir were to read this, would he believe it? How would he know that what I am seeing is real?

Maybe I could offer photography as proof that what I am seeing is genuine. But can a handful of ducks, fish, and small bugs really be called bustling wildlife to him? After a small sip of my coffee (Nescafe, not sponsored), I have decided that to me, this counts as a thriving ecosystem, simply because it is what I see as bustling wildlife.

Sir might disagree. He may not see this as a lively ecosystem at all, only because his idea of "bustling with wildlife" differs from mine. Still, both views are opinions, shaped by our own experiences, and both are true to each of us in our own way.

What we can agree on is that there is real life outside this window. Ducks waddle to and from the pond hunting for fish or insects, ants march past the windowsill carrying scraps of food in their mandibles, and fish slip deeper into the water to escape the ducks above.

This makes me truly wonder: is truth always more important than opinion? I look outside again, searching for an answer, and eventually land on no. Think of it this way: if someone asks what your favorite hobbies are, that question calls for an opinion, because it reveals who you are, not simply a fact about you.

Still, opinions are shaped by truth. If we rely only on opinions and never seek what is true, we end up convincing no one but ourselves, because every opinion needs some foundation of truth to stand on. In life, even something as simple as sitting here with my coffee, there is a time and a place for everything.

Swimming Through Reality: Why the Existence of Facts is Important amidst the Sea of Feelings

How do we know the truth?

Through decades and centuries of our existence, humanities have faced situations where they have to define reality, break it apart, reconstruct and form a definite and tangible proof through logic and question to pursue the answer of one question: What makes something fundamentally true?

Knowing the truth, one must find the logic and evidence to support it. Evidence can be through observation, logically aligned and lastly, the most important of all: a repeatable outcome. One can say, gravity is true if they release an object they are holding and it falls downwards, proving the logic and existence of gravity to be true.

How is truth different from opinion?

The boundary that exist between truth and opinion all points towards Objectivity vs. Subjectivity.

Truth could be outside personal bias, aka Objectivity. This fact could be verified, tested, or be even disproven by greater truth as you wander further than what previous known knowledge shows.

Opinions reflect personal judgement and preference or interpretation. While they can be backed up and supported by truth, it can still be debated, disagreed upon and above all else, never alter the underlying facts.

Is Truth Always Superior?

Truth will never be above or below opinion as choosing one over the other requires context.

Objective truths are the building blocks or the very basis humanity relies upon, be it from medicine, science and law; without it, we would fall and humanity would collapse, turning into a lawless, brainless, hopeless desolate place. But if we also neglect opinions, choosing them as the almighty, we would lack the subjectivity, the color this world needs since creativity comes from opinions being built on top of opinions like art styles and color preference. Artists of any kind don't follow one strict agreed upon rule, they make their own, that's where opinions truly shine.

So to settle the debate on which is better, the answer is: Both of them can never be better than the other. They are parts of a system that constantly relies on each other to further improve and create. Opinions are the soul, and the soul needs the body and mind to function, so the truth becomes the body to make the opinions have structure.

What is a world without truth? Only opinion?

If the very existence of truth vanishes entirely, this world would immediately descend into the most realistic definition of hades. Medical treatment would regress into total guess work without factual truth to back up remedy, The Law would be corrupt, since the judge can sentence anyone they dislike into more than 10+ years of whatever sentence they give, and without a shared baseline of facts, meaningful debate becomes impossible because there is no neutral ground to stand on.

Power over reason, that's what dictates reality. And the quote "History is written by the winners" By Winston Churchill would be the statement that describes the whole ordeal".

What exactly is the truth about truth? There are many philosophies regarding this particular question ranging from the earliest Greek eras to the modern day inquiries; also with its own overarching questions on which is the real philosophy about what is or what is not truth? All in all it is quite the dilemma in which yours, your truth is grounded upon. However, maybe that’s not the real question we should be asking maybe the real question lies on how you are gonna live with that truth of yours and what will you do if it falls apart. When your belief falls apart. When your own identity is questioned. When you witness your entirety fall face flat on the ground. What truth exactly is greater? For even lies themselves become false truths and truths false lies.

We begin to question ourselves if what we believe we live by is reality or just a made up construct to supply the desire of searching for grounded truth. However, more than correspondence, pragmatic or what else truth is belief in one’s choice – in my opinion. Why? Why do I say so? Is it even true? To be honest I don't know myself, yet I believe it's true. Yes most people would disagree, and I don’t blame them for doing so since that in itself is also evidence for truth based on self-knowledge or standards. But before diving in my side of this argument, it is best if we first try to acknowledge the theories of the past about truth, particularly: correspondence theory, pragmatic theory, and coherence theory.

correspondence theory. This theory believes that the nature of truth is defined to the reality it aligns with. The existence of a real subject, event or phenomenon that aligns with a particular claim is considered to be real or true since it does not contradict with real-life (Khatchadourian, 2011).

Pragmatic Theory. States that one subject can be classified as real or true if it has a consequence or result. If for instance an experiment yielded results whether or not it is good or bad, this is enough evidence to identify the test as truth. Note that this is not merely focused on the scientific field or what not rather encompasses both humanities and politics (Pierce, 2019).

Coherence Theory. This theory highlights the pre-requisites or prepositions as the ground for truth, this means it is true if it revolves around an existing belief or system that was introduced beforehand (Young, 2018).

Now that we are able to layout our understanding about truth through various theories. We move towards reflecting on these questions. Final Output: How do we know truth? How is truth different from opinion? Is truth always superior to an opinion? What would the world be like if there were no truth, only opinion?

We know truth based on how we relate to it, whether with ourselves, others, or something like that... All in all truth correlates to our identity, what we believe is true based on our principles and ideologies in life. Although this idea is somewhat similar to opinion, there is a clear difference between the two. Primarily, what they seek to prove. One seeks expression of self, the other aims to bring clarity and complete absence of dishonesty in some degree because not everyone will accept the hurt, but the ones who did are braver than one could fathom.

Fathom...?

Where were we, oh right...

Although most would agree that truth is superior over opinion, it's not always the case. Some people would choose to believe opinion over truth. In all honesty, it's all about sounding confident and persuasive atleast that's what the folks say.

But...

Can you just imagine the world without truth? In my case, I did. I saw it as nothing, absent, but what even is absence if there is no truth to identify it in the first place. The absence of truth could be just the nonexistence of everything from the highest degree to the lowest or the other way around. From the lack of the laws of physics to your simple white lies. It always returns to nothing.

Isn't that terrifying.

Nature / Environment

Is nature ours to own, or does it have a purpose entirely its own? It is a rich question, one most people taste once and rarely return to for a second sip.

do humans own nature? No, and I mean that firmly. We do not own nature, for the same reason we do not own our family or friends. It exists for its own sake, not to serve us, having been here long before humanity learned to use its resources for our benefit. We coexist with nature rather than possess it.

Just as children depend on their parents to grow, we depend on nature: freshwater, food that has sustained us for centuries, and materials to build shelter against the elements. But does that dependence mean we own our provider? Of course not. What nature gives us demands care in return, along with responsibility and accountability.

Let me share a story that captures this idea. A mother gave her son a week's allowance for school. He spent it all in a single day on things that had nothing to do with school, then had the nerve to ask for more. Being gentle and a little too trusting, she gave in.

This went on for days, until Thursday came and he needed money again, this time for snacks and a class trip on Friday. Just before he knocked on her door, he heard her crying softly.

He paused and looked through the keyhole instead. Inside, his mother was photographing her clothes for an online resale site.

It hit him instantly: the money had never been for his enjoyment. It was for necessity. Now he watched his mother selling her belongings, from her favorite outfits to her own grandmother's clothes, just to keep giving him what he asked for.

He rushed in and confessed everything about his careless spending. She scolded him, but gently enough that the lesson would stick. He carried it with him from then on: having the ability to spend something does not mean you own it. Power like that always comes with responsibility.

The same is true of nature. We are allowed to use what it offers, but we never truly own it; everything is borrowed. We live alongside it, shape it, and are shaped by it in return. Yet these days, that sense of care seems to be fading. We have made progress over the decades, but not nearly enough, and people continue to grow more careless with time.

Van Gogh once suggested that life without the courage to try anything would hardly be worth living. I hope we never have to test that idea with nature, our oldest and most enduring partner. So use what it gives you wisely, and never take it for granted, because even the hero Spider-Man says that with great power comes with great responsibility.

Earth's Value: Seeking Beyond the Lens and Exploring Worth Beyond Resource

Beyond Human Utility: Does Nature Have Value on Its Own?

Centuries of the rise and fall of many civilizations, humankind viewed through a practical lens, viewing Earth as a massive resource to utilize to survive. Forests and trees for wood to create shelter and tools, rivers being used as an energy source by modern technology, and even land itself is terraformed to satisfy the desires of the growing need for expansion so the growing population will always have a place to call home.

That said, with all of humanity relying on earth purely as a service provider for mankind begs a question: does the natural world exist solely for us, or does it possess worth simply by being alive?

Instrumental Value: Nature as a Resource

When the perspective shifts and is viewed through the scope of Humanitarian-centric ideals, the value of nature is strictly measured by what it can provide for the survival and comfort for Humans. Nature provides life support through giving fresh water, breathable air, and fertile soil, all essential for the survival of every human, and if one of these things were not provided, we would all cease to exist.

Nature also provides an impact in the economy through raw materials supply, be it from animals, harvests or even minerals, there is no doubt that nature has prints on our current economy, and without it, the basic building blocks of every products, machine, trades and etc., the entire economy would halt or at least slow down at a rapid pace.

Under these lenses, humans see environmental protection being crucial if losing a habitat hurts human prosperity and survival.

Intrinsic Value: Worth Beyond Human Use

A more ecocentric mindset argues that nature does not only provide value as a well of resource, but it also provides intrinsic value, value that is beyond what the human eye can see.

Nature is history. Every part of it existed before mankind saw it as a resource provider. Coral reefs, rainforests, and complexes. Ecosystems thrived for billions of years before humans arrived and do not depend on human validation to have purpose

Nature is home. Humans are not the only living beings on earth. Mammals, fiishes, insects need Nature to have a place to live in, be it hollow trees, under rocks or anybody of waters, humans must always consider our fellow neighbors since we share Nature with them. Every living organism, from soil microbes to apex predators, plays a role in a self-sustaining system that exists for its own continuity.

Recognition of the worth of Nature and Earth as a whole, not only seeing what it could provide, but the other values that can be tangible and intangible is a way to be able to appreciate our own planet. That is beyond what the human eye can see. When we see a forest or ocean not as a bucket of resources to deploy, but as a living community with its own right to flourish, environmental protection becomes an obligation rather than convenience.

Does nature exist only for humans, or does it have values on its own?

“This is honestly quite an interesting topic, and although with the previous times I have written articles somehow I love the style of writing what I want to try and say no matter the length or form– of course I would like to maintain the proper use of grammar, nonetheless, nothing beats the freedom to write.”

Recently, while trying to search up a good article to read about stuff (things that would interest me) I stumbled on the article talking about the intrinsic and extrinsic values.

What’s the difference?

Well… intrinsic values primarily focus more on the innate nature of objects, events, or in this case beings. On the other hand, extrinsic values –you guess it– refers to external qualities.

Now that we laid that off let us focus on the subject matter. What are the values of nature, what it means to us, and does it have its own values or exist to serve only us?

Nature existed long before all of us did, ever since the beginning of time or at least that is what most people agree on… it serves as a sustainer for all beings on earth, everything that the horizon can see. What does it take to be a sustainer of all beings?

Just imagine being a mother to all needing to supply both the needs and wants of your children, and that the number of offspring you have is numerous, uncountable. With so many different species you can just imagine what it takes to take care of everybody with little to no avail of even brushing your hair straight in the morning everyday. With all the work that nature does for our lives, truly it has the qualities of a great mother. Thus, as the children of nature, it is our duty to live out the life given– to not simply seek our own desires, but for all of us to unite, to care for each other, to uplift each other, and to not blindly “follow your heart,” and to look around you, to look back, up, and forward and realize what needs to change, to be changed.

The times nowadays are terrible, there are many things happening whether good or bad, and we fail to see what matters. Let us change that. I think that would be terrific!
